# Account security and help desk connectors

New registration collects first/last name, email, birth date, international-format contact phone, legal company name, billing address, ISO country and tax ID. Birth dates and phone numbers are not returned in the general user projection and are not sent to AI or external ticket connectors. Tax IDs are collected as supplied; Stripe Checkout still validates the applicable billing/tax information. The trial lasts twelve calendar days from account creation.

Account security is available from the header, for every role, and offered immediately after signup. Email uses the account email address and the existing SMTP outbox. Google Authenticator-compatible TOTP is an alternative. Enrollment requires the current password and proof of the second factor. Enabling MFA revokes other signed-in sessions. Login grants no authenticated user session until the challenge passes. Challenges expire after ten minutes, allow at most five attempts, and TOTP steps cannot be reused. Eight single-use recovery codes are shown once after enrollment; save them outside the account. Disabling requires the current password and a second-factor code. Email confirmation during enrollment also verifies the account email. Forgotten-password recovery preserves MFA and invalidates pending challenges. SMS/WhatsApp are intentionally not supported.

Encryption uses AES-256-GCM with `DATA_ENCRYPTION_KEY`, falling back to the existing stable `SESSION_SECRET`. Keep this key stable, private and included in the hosting secret recovery procedure. Rotation requires re-encrypting existing TOTP and connector secrets first. Never commit secret values. Email delivery requires SMTP_URL, MAIL_FROM and PUBLIC_APP_URL; OTP bodies are redacted after sending and expired pending messages are discarded.

Overdue alerts are enabled by default, configurable in workspace settings, and require the existing mail service. The worker runs every minute. Active open/in-progress overdue tickets notify their active, email-verified assignee; unassigned tickets notify active verified administrators of that workspace. The tenant must have full access and cannot be a demo workspace. At most one alert per ticket, recipient and UTC date is queued. Pending alerts are checked again before delivery against the current status, assignment, verified recipient and workspace settings. Resolved tickets stop generating alerts. Existing SLA escalation rules remain separate. Recipients receive the ticket title and an authenticated application link, not internal notes or attachments.

Zendesk and ServiceNow configuration is available to workspace administrators from “Ticket integrations”. No credentials are preconfigured and no automatic synchronization runs. Enter the instance hostname and an OAuth access token with ticket permissions; save, test the connection, and explicitly authorize each ticket export. Tokens are stored encrypted separately from public workspace settings and are never returned to the browser. Only HTTPS single-subdomain `*.zendesk.com` and `*.service-now.com` hosts are accepted; HTTP redirects are rejected. Connections and record mappings are tenant-scoped.

This first connector version exports the title, description, priority/urgency and supported state fields. Zendesk's initial comment is private. Internal notes, attachments and personal user profiles are excluded. Subsequent exports update the mapped remote record. ServiceNow state is deliberately not overwritten because instance workflows differ. It is a manual, one-way integration; inbound webhooks, attachment synchronization and background bidirectional reconciliation are not implemented. OAuth refresh/renewal is manual. After an ambiguous provider timeout, check the external system before retrying: the remote write may have succeeded before the response was lost.

API references: [Zendesk Ticketing API](https://developer.zendesk.com/documentation/ticketing/getting-started/zendesk-api-quick-start/), [Zendesk OAuth](https://developer.zendesk.com/documentation/authentication/api-tokens-to-oauth/), [ServiceNow REST API](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/api-reference/rest-api-explorer/c_RESTAPI.html).

Nine interface and reporting languages are bundled as local JSON dictionaries: English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese, Russian and Danish. Translations are prepared offline; the browser does not send customer text to a translation service. Names, ticket descriptions, custom data and uploaded files retain their original language. Legal reading translations include the original English reference and require legal review before regulated production use.
